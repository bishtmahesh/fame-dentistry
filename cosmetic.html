Our Approach

Never cosmetic for the sake of cosmetic.

At FAME, we don't offer a menu of treatments and ask which you'd like. We assess what's clinically sound, and then explore what's aesthetically desirable — in that order.

This matters because the most common regret in cosmetic dentistry isn't the treatment chosen — it's the treatment that wasn't assessed properly first. A veneer over an untreated bite issue will fail. A whitening course that ignores erosion will cause harm.

FAME's cosmetic work is led by Dr Tariq Bashir, whose background in restorative dentistry means every aesthetic decision is grounded in structural integrity.

Ceramic Veneers

Thin. Strong. Permanent.

Porcelain veneers crafted to your tooth anatomy — not to a generic template. We work with a specialist ceramist. Preparation is minimised wherever possible.

Composite Bonding

Reversible. Immediate. Precise.

Direct composite applied chairside in a single visit. Best suited to minor chips, shape corrections, and closing small gaps — without permanent tooth reduction.

Longevity depends on home care and occlusion. Annual review recommended.

Tooth Whitening

Clinical. Not cosmetic-counter.

Prescription-strength take-home whitening, fitted to custom trays. Always preceded by an erosion and sensitivity assessment. We don't sell whitening without assessing suitability first.

Not offered in isolation — always part of a full assessment appointment.

Our Philosophy

Smile design with a clinician, not a technician.

"The best cosmetic outcome is one you can't see has been done — because it looks exactly right for that face."

Dr Tariq Bashir's restorative background means cosmetic cases at FAME start with occlusion, proportion, and structural integrity — not with a shade guide. Aesthetics follow function, not the other way around.

We do not offer 'smile transformations' or 'Hollywood smiles'. If that's the language you've encountered elsewhere, we'd encourage you to consider whether a practice that uses it is the right one for a permanent change to your teeth.

Who This Is For

Is cosmetic treatment right for you?

Good candidates include
  • Adults unhappy with the colour, shape, or symmetry of their smile
  • Patients with chipped or worn front teeth
  • Those who've had composite bonding fail and want a longer-lasting option
  • Patients considering veneers for the first time
  • Anyone who wants to understand their options before committing
Important to know first
  • Active tooth decay or gum disease must be treated before cosmetic work proceeds
  • Bruxism (teeth grinding) affects which treatments are appropriate
  • Cosmetic work is not available on the NHS — full fee transparency at consultation
  • Some changes require a staged approach over several appointments

The Digital Advantage in Implant Dentistry

Planning with greater insight before treatment begins.

Successful implant dentistry relies on meticulous preparation as much as clinical expertise. Before any treatment is recommended, every aspect of your oral health must be carefully assessed to determine the most appropriate approach for your individual needs.

At FAME Dentistry, our digital implant workflow provides a comprehensive understanding of your teeth, jawbone, surrounding anatomical structures, and bite relationships. Three-dimensional imaging and digital planning tools allow your clinician to evaluate available bone, identify important structures such as nerves and sinuses, and consider the ideal restorative outcome before treatment begins.

This level of planning supports informed clinical decisions and enables every stage of treatment to be coordinated with greater accuracy. Rather than relying solely on traditional impressions or two-dimensional images, digital workflows provide a complete clinical picture that supports personalised care and thoughtful treatment planning.

Technology does not replace clinical judgement. It enhances it by providing detailed information that helps our clinicians deliver precise, evidence-based implant care with confidence.

The Technology Behind Digital Implant Workflow

Every digital tool contributes to a more informed treatment experience.

Digital Assessment and Diagnosis

High-resolution intraoral scans and detailed diagnostic imaging capture accurate records of your teeth, gums, jaw, and surrounding oral structures, providing the foundation for comprehensive implant planning.

Best for: Assessing suitability for dental implant treatment.

Three-Dimensional Treatment Planning

Advanced 3D imaging allows your clinician to evaluate bone volume, bone quality, neighbouring teeth, nerves, and sinus anatomy before treatment begins.

Best for: Single implants, multiple implants, and full-arch rehabilitation.

Virtual Implant Positioning

Digital planning software enables implant placement to be mapped virtually before treatment, helping determine the most appropriate position based on your anatomy and restorative objectives.

Best for: Creating personalised treatment plans with restorative precision.

Digital Impression Technology

Comfortable digital scanning captures highly accurate impressions for implant-supported crowns, bridges, and full-arch restorations while reducing the need for conventional impression materials in many cases.

Best for: Designing accurately fitting implant restorations.

Long-Term Digital Monitoring

Digital records provide an ongoing reference for future reviews, allowing your clinician to monitor implant health, surrounding tissues, and treatment stability over time.

Best for: Supporting long-term implant maintenance and preventive care.

Questions

Helping you make informed decisions.

What is a digital implant workflow?

A digital implant workflow combines technologies such as intraoral scanning, three-dimensional imaging, and computer-assisted treatment planning to support every stage of dental implant treatment, from diagnosis through to the final restoration.

How does digital implant workflow benefit implant treatment?

Digital workflows provide detailed clinical information that supports careful assessment and personalised treatment planning. This helps your clinician evaluate your oral anatomy and develop an approach based on your individual needs.

Is digital implant planning suitable for every patient?

Digital planning is beneficial for many patients considering dental implants. Following your assessment, your clinician will determine which technologies are appropriate for your treatment.

Are digital scans more comfortable than traditional impressions?

Many patients find digital intraoral scans more comfortable than conventional impressions, particularly if impression materials trigger discomfort or a gag reflex.

Can digital implant workflow be used for complex implant treatment?

Yes. Digital planning can support a wide range of implant procedures, including single tooth replacement, multiple implants, and full-arch rehabilitation where clinically appropriate.

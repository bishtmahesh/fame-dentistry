By Dr Ferhan Ahmed

The most important decision in implant treatment isn't clinical. It's who you trust.

Why This Guide

Get this decision right, and almost everything else follows.

I want to start with something you may not expect a dentist to say: the most important decision you'll make in the whole implant process has nothing to do with the procedure itself. It's the decision about who you trust to carry it out. Get it right, and the rest tends to follow. Get it wrong, and the cost — financial, physical and emotional — can be significant and long-lasting.

Over the years, many of my patients came to me after a difficult experience elsewhere. Some had lost money, some had lost time, a few had lost both and were starting over. What struck me was that their mistakes were rarely about dentistry. They were about due diligence. There were signals they'd missed, and the questions they didn't think to ask until, with hindsight, it was too late.

So I wrote down the seven mistakes I see people making most often along with the specific questions you should be asking to ensure you don't make them too. — Dr Ferhan Ahmed

What's Inside

Seven mistakes. Seven questions to avoid them.

The guide identifies each mistake and gives you the one question to ask a practice to ensure you don't get caught out.

Judging a practice by its own marketing. Most dental practices have a beautiful website and glowing reviews nowadays, but they tell you far less than you'd think. I'll give you the one question that cuts straight through the polish.
Being seduced by an offer that seems too good to be true. Headline prices, countdowns, “teeth in a day.” The guide explains what these usually conceal and how to test a price properly.
Dismissing the small things at your first visit. The way a practice behaves when it thinks it doesn't count tells you exactly how it will behave when it does. Here's what to watch for before you ever sit in the chair.
Treating implant surgery as something to combine with a holiday abroad. For implants specifically, there are three questions that will inform your decision making over whether it's actually worth looking at getting advanced dental work like implants abroad.
Assuming one implant dentist is much like another. The range of ability is wider than most people realise. Three questions separate genuine experience from years-on-a-certificate.
Not asking what happens after your treatment. Most patients ask about before and during. The character of a practice shows in what it does after the invoice is paid. Of course, I'll give you the questions that will give you your answer in advance.
Deciding before you're ready — or feeling pressured to do so. The pressure signals to walk away from, and the standard of consideration any practice worth choosing will offer you without being asked.

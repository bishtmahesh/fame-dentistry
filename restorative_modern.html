The FAME Approach

Function is the foundation. Aesthetics follow.

Restorative dentistry at FAME isn't a menu of procedures. It's a clinical problem-solving process that begins with understanding what your teeth need to do — and designing a solution that will last.

Dr Tariq Bashir trained in restorative dentistry with a focus on diagnosis and treatment planning. His view: the most common cause of repeated dental work is a treatment plan that addressed the symptom without understanding the cause. Every FAME restorative plan begins with that understanding.

Whether you need a single crown or a full-mouth rehabilitation, the standard is the same: understand the problem fully, plan comprehensively, and execute with precision.

What We Treat

Restorative treatments at FAME.

01.

Crowns & Bridges

Full-coverage ceramic crowns and fixed bridges designed to function identically to natural teeth. Planned as part of a comprehensive treatment, not as a standalone quick fix.

02.

Inlays & Onlays

Conservative ceramic restorations for teeth too damaged for a filling but not yet requiring a full crown. Preserves more natural tooth structure than conventional alternatives.

03.

Full Mouth Rehabilitation

Comprehensive restorative planning for patients with extensive wear, multiple missing teeth, or a history of repeated treatment. Planned across both arches with function and aesthetics in balance.

04.

Composite Bonding

Direct resin restorations for chipped, worn, or misshapen teeth. At FAME, bonding is treatment-planned — not applied without a full assessment of bite and existing wear patterns.

05.

Bite Assessment & Occlusal Therapy

Diagnosis and management of bite problems — including TMJ dysfunction, tooth wear from grinding, and failed previous restorations caused by occlusal imbalance.

06.

Treatment Planning for Referred Patients

Comprehensive restorative assessments for patients referred by other practices. Written treatment plan provided. Patient returns to the referring practice for ongoing care.

Questions

What patients ask before starting treatment.

I've had a lot of dental work that hasn't lasted. Can you help?

Yes — and this is one of the most common presentations at FAME. The first consultation is focused on understanding why previous treatment hasn't held, before any new work is proposed. In most cases, the answer is identifiable and addressable.

How is a FAME crown different from a crown at another practice?

The crown itself is similar — the difference is in the planning. At FAME, a crown is never placed without a bite assessment, a review of the opposing teeth, and an understanding of what caused the tooth to fail in the first place. This is what determines whether it lasts.

What does full mouth rehabilitation involve?

It begins with a comprehensive assessment appointment — photographs, study models, bite analysis, and a review of existing work. From this, a phased treatment plan is created. Treatment is sequenced to address functional issues first, then aesthetics. Timelines vary but are always planned transparently from the start.

I grind my teeth. Should I see you before having cosmetic work done?

Yes — this is important. Unmanaged bruxism causes cosmetic restorations to fail, often within a few years. Dr Bashir's restorative assessment will identify the extent of wear and advise on management before any cosmetic treatment proceeds.

Who This Is For

Is restorative treatment right for you?

Good candidates include
  • Patients with a history of repeated dental work that hasn't held
  • Anyone with significant tooth wear from grinding or acid erosion
  • Patients needing one or more crowns or bridges
  • Those with missing teeth planning for implants or bridges
  • Patients considering full mouth rehabilitation
  • Anyone with an unresolved bite problem or TMJ discomfort
What to bring
  • Records of previous dental work if available
  • X-rays or scans from other practices
  • A description of what has failed and when
  • Your medication list if you have relevant conditions
  • A clear sense of what you want to achieve — function, aesthetics, or both
